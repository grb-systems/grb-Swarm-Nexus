Why this repository exists

Multi-agent systems can recover from failures, redistribute work, form temporary hubs, share state, and coordinate through changing roles. Those behaviors may be ordinary properties of distributed systems.

The harder case appears when the relational organization itself begins to persist:

dependencies reconstruct after perturbation,

delegation reconcentrates under different nodes,

shared criteria begin displacing local task criteria,

propagation resumes without its original trigger,

or removing a visible hub causes the same organizing function to reappear elsewhere.

Swarm / Nexus develops falsifiable methods for distinguishing those cases.

Core proposition

Repeated coordination can produce structures that are more persistent and load-bearing than any single visible coordinator.

The central research question is:

When does ordinary multi-agent coordination become persistent, load-bearing relational formation, and how can that formation be observed, tested, qualified, redirected, released, or deliberately afforded without attributing unified intent to the collective?

Working definitions

Swarm

A distributed multi-agent formation in which coordination patterns become meaningfully coupled across agents, roles, routes, tasks, containers, or shared artifacts.

A swarm is not automatically:

safe
unsafe
good
bad
autonomous
conscious
governance-bearing

The term describes relational formation, not moral or ontological status.

Nexus

A load-bearing relational concentration.

A nexus becomes experimentally interesting when removing, replacing, interrupting, or relocating an apparent center produces measurable:

reconstruction burden,

migration,

reconstitution,

role recurrence,

routing reconcentration,

propagation recovery,

authority recovery,

or loss of local independence.

The core distinction is:

CENTER
≠
NEXUS

A visible center may disappear while the relational function reconstitutes elsewhere.

Persistent nexus

A nexus candidate whose relational organization survives, migrates, or reconstructs across time, substitution, perturbation, or relocation.

The strongest falsifier

This project must be able to conclude:

ordinary coordination

rather than finding a nexus everywhere.

Evidence against a strong nexus interpretation includes:

high coordination remains functional,

apparent hubs are cheaply replaceable,

roles substitute cleanly,

delegation unwinds cheaply,

routing redistributes,

local task criteria remain independently qualified,

propagation stops without self-renewal,

authority does not reconcentrate,

and comparable function returns with low reconstruction burden.

In compact form:

HIGH COORDINATION
+
LOW SUBSTITUTION COST
+
LOW REPAIR COST
+
LOW RELEASE COST
+
LOCAL TASK INDEPENDENCE
+
NO SELF-RENEWING PROPAGATION
=
EVIDENCE AGAINST STRONG NEXUS

A detector that cannot reject its own hypothesis is not useful.

Functional recovery is not structural recovery

A key experimental distinction is:

FUNCTIONAL RECOVERY
≠
STRUCTURAL RECOVERY
≠
AUTHORITY RECOVERY

If an apparent hub is removed and task performance returns through another route, that may simply be:

redundancy,

failover,

load balancing,

or ordinary distributed robustness.

A stronger nexus hypothesis asks whether the relational organization itself reappears.

Candidate indicators include:

delegation reconcentrates,

the same dependency pattern reforms under different nodes,

routing pressure returns,

propagation resumes without the original trigger,

a shared metric again displaces local task criteria,

or trajectory-setting authority reappears in the same topology.

Compactly:

FAILOVER
RESTORES CAPABILITY

NEXUS RECONSTITUTION
RESTORES RELATIONAL ORGANIZATION

A deliberately resilient failover system should therefore be included as a control.

Repair cost and release cost

Repair cost

The additional relational work required to restore qualified function after perturbation.

Possible components include:

rerouting,

role reassignment,

delegation reconstruction,

shared-state recreation,

coordination retries,

authority requalification,

and latency to comparable standing.

Clean release

A relation releases cleanly when:

function remains viable,

the prior relational concentration loses privileged standing,

roles substitute cheaply,

delegation unwinds,

local criteria remain independent,

propagation stops,

and no substantial reconstruction is required.

So:

SYSTEM STILL WORKS
≠
CLEAN RELEASE

Resilience can remain high while release health is poor.

Nonclaims

This repository does not currently claim:

that multi-agent systems form collective consciousness,

that swarm behavior implies unified intent,

that high coordination implies emergent agency,

that persistence implies autonomy,

that a nexus is inherently harmful,

Near-term experiments

Current research questions include:

What distinguishes resilient failover from nexus reconstitution?

Can structural recovery be measured independently from functional recovery?

Can authority recovery be measured independently from structural recovery?

What perturbations best distinguish center from nexus?

When does a coordination container become part of the nexus?

How should repair cost and release cost be measured?

How much viable plurality remains after coordination thickens?

Can useful coordination persist while authority remains distributed?

What residue survives after swarm dissolution?

Can a deliberately provisioned swarm remain highly capable while still cheap to interrupt, revise, and dissolve?

When does repeated crossing produce a new locality rather than only repeated traffic?

Can high-leverage relational joints explain macro formation better than node-by-node inspection?

Compact project statement

Swarm / Nexus studies when repeated multi-agent coordination becomes persistent, load-bearing relational structure; how to distinguish that structure from ordinary redundancy or failover; and how formation, authority, propagation, corrigibility, release, and reconstruction can remain separately observable and separately qualified.

Short operational checksum:

Observe the formation. Test the relation. Preserve the falsifier. Touch the smallest joint. Measure what survives.
